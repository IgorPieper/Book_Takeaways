# 2. How to learn

---

Book: "Włam się do Mózgu" [Hack Your Brain] by Radek Kotarski. As far as I know, as of today 18.08.2026, the book has only been published in Polish.

## Introduction

---

There are many ways to learn. Some are effective, while others are less so. We are familiar with many of them, but we often do not explore which ones work best. For me, this book provided a useful summary and clarified how to learn effectively.

These methods can be combined and selected depending on your needs and which approaches work best for you.

## A Way to Learn Easily

---

You read the material once and immediately move on to checking what you remember. You adapt the form of testing to your needs. For example:

- You cover a section and recall it from memory.
- You teach another person what you have learned.
- Another person quizzes you.

> Personally, I used a method called “π-station” to learn vocabulary in another language. I took a lined notebook and assigned one word to each line. I wrote the word repeatedly until I reached the end of the line. Each time I wrote it, I said its meaning aloud and pronounced the word.
>
> After completing the page, I used another notebook or a page farther ahead to test which words I remembered. I marked every word I forgot or misspelled with a minus sign. Then I wrote each of those words repeatedly for another full line and tested myself again. I repeated this process until I remembered all of them.

## Ways to Strengthen Our Learning

---

1. Taking notes. Write them in your own words based on what you have learned. They do not have to be clear to others. What matters is that you understand them. Mix different formats, using whatever suits you best at the moment. Write paragraphs, add drawings, create mind maps and design flashcards.

2. Use gestures. Talk about what you have learned while using a variety of gestures.

3. Engage different senses. Connect each part of the material with a different stimulus. You can add emotions, smells, touch, and visual elements. The stimuli can be real or imagined. Use visualization to move the information you want to remember into imagined worlds that combine different senses. Learn history in a sensory-rich café, biology in a park, and languages by taking an imaginary trip to another country.

4. Surround yourself with the subject you are learning. Watch related videos, read posts about the topic, and change the language on your phone.

5. Explore each topic more deeply. Do not limit yourself to what is required. You will remember a topic much more easily if you ask questions and search for answers, such as: Why does it work this way? How does it work? What caused it? What are its consequences? How does it connect to what I already know? Can I explain it using an example? What would happen if one element changed?

6. Focus. Limit distractions while studying.

7. Get enough sleep. Pulling an all-nighter will only harm you.

8. Take breaks, but make sure you actually rest during them. Go for a walk, meditate, or take a shower. Do not clutter your mind with additional stimuli, such as social media.

---
