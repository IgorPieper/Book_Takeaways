# 11. How to Get Started

---

Book: "Jak zacząć" [How to Get Started] by Marcin Osman. As far as I know, as of today 21.11.2026, the book has only been published in Polish.

## Live on Your Own Terms

---

Do not shape your entire life around work or other people's expectations. First, decide how you want to live. Only then choose how to earn money and organize your daily life. Write down the conditions you refuse to accept, such as rigid working hours, frequent business travel, or having to manage a large team. Then reverse that list to describe your ideal conditions.

Gradually eliminate what conflicts with those conditions. Instead of passively reacting to circumstances, actively look for and create opportunities. You can make conscious exceptions to your rules, but do not let those exceptions become your everyday reality.

## Start Before You Feel Ready

---

Do not wait for the perfect moment or complete knowledge. Spend a few hours researching, talk to someone with practical experience, and then make your first attempt. Only through action will you discover which skills you actually lack. Do not collect advice that you never put to use.

If you keep putting off a task, ask whether it genuinely moves you toward your goal. Eliminate unnecessary tasks. Start important ones immediately, setting a short deadline or changing your environment if that helps you focus. Your first result needs to exist, not be perfect.

## Turn Dreams into Concrete Steps

---

Write down your dreams and rate their difficulty on a scale from 1 to 10. Arrange them from easiest to hardest, and break large goals into stages. If you dream of traveling for several months, begin with a weekend trip, then a week, and eventually a month.

For each goal, find someone who has achieved something similar. Ask how they started, what mistakes they made, and what they would do differently. Learn from their experience, but test their methods for yourself.

## Build Healthy Habits One Small Step at a Time

---

First, understand your starting point: track or photograph your meals for a week, examine your habits, and discuss any necessary medical tests with a doctor. Then gradually improve your diet and choose an activity you can do consistently.

Do not force yourself into a sport just because it is popular. List what puts you off a particular activity, turn those obstacles into requirements, and find something better suited to you. Start with one short walk or workout. Once that feels easy, gradually increase how often and how long you exercise. Build the habit first, then raise the demands.

## Simplify Responsibilities and Reclaim Your Time

---

Review your household responsibilities and decide which ones you can eliminate, simplify, combine, or delegate. Useful tools, weekly shopping with a list, and grouping similar tasks into time blocks can all help.

In a relationship, divide responsibilities clearly, taking into account each person's current workload and need for rest. Separate time for work, family, and recovery. Designate spaces for specific activities to make it easier to focus on them.

## Gain Experiences Outside Your Routine

---

If you want to travel, list the places you want to visit and why. Set a budget and a date for a short nearby trip, then gradually plan journeys farther away. You do not need to organize a major adventure to begin.

If you are learning a language, surround yourself with it every day: change the language on your devices, watch content in that language, and speak regularly with people who use it. You will gain the most when learning becomes part of everyday life.

## Practice Courage and Set Boundaries

---

Regularly take small, safe actions that make you uncomfortable: start a conversation with a stranger, ask for a discount, or try something new. This develops initiative and helps you become more comfortable with the possibility of rejection.

At the same time, decide what matters to you. If someone's request conflicts with your priorities, time, or boundaries, say no clearly and calmly. Assertiveness does not mean rejecting people. It means consciously choosing what you agree to.

## Build Relationships Before You Need Them

---

At events, listen more than you talk about yourself. Ask people about their work, interests, and challenges. If you can help them or introduce them to someone who can, do so without expecting an immediate return.

After a good conversation, promptly send a short message thanking them and sharing your contact details. Following through on such a small promise helps build trust. Do not judge people solely by their appearance, individual gestures, or first impressions.

## Validate Demand Before Building a Business

---

Do not start by registering a company, buying equipment, or perfecting a website. Describe your offer in one sentence, find potential customers, and check whether they are willing to pay. Interest alone is not enough: look for actual orders, payments, or other concrete commitments.

You can test an idea on a small scale, even by selling an existing product before investing in your own. Not every passion needs to become a business. Only repeatable sales provide a basis for further investment and growth.

## Find Your First Customers in Your Existing Network

---

Start with a list of friends and contacts. Identify people who might genuinely need your product and ask whether they would be interested in buying it. When your offer is ready, return with a specific proposal and price.

Also talk to new potential customers at events, during presentations, and in communities related to your industry. Do not judge your progress by compliments. Track how many people actually buy, and improve your offer based on those results.

## Choose Business Partners and Delegate Carefully

---

Before starting a business with someone, ask yourself whether you really need a partner. If you do, complete a small project together first. See whether you can make decisions, resolve conflicts, and honor your commitments.

Start delegating with small tasks too. Instead of handing over an entire project, assign one part and assess the quality of the work, communication, and reliability. Look for people who are more skilled than you in their respective fields. The goal is to free up time for the work where you create the most value.

## Organize Your Work So You Can Do It Remotely

---

List your responsibilities and meetings from the past month. Eliminate activities that add no value, then assess which remaining tasks can be handled online. Replace unnecessary in-person meetings with remote calls.

If you are employed, first propose working remotely one day per week. Demonstrate your results, then negotiate additional remote days. Change your work arrangements gradually rather than trying to rebuild everything at once.

## Create and Publish Without Perfectionism

---

Want to start a blog, YouTube channel, podcast, or professional profile? First, determine who you are creating for and what problem you are solving. List questions people often ask you, stories you tell, and topics drawn from your own experience. Choose your first pieces of content from that list.

A phone, good lighting, and clear audio are enough to start. Do not spend weeks on a name, studio, intro, expensive equipment, or complicated editing. Record, publish, gather feedback, and improve the next piece. Your content should be useful, and its quality good enough to consume comfortably.

## Build Direct Connections with Your Audience

---

Do not rely solely on social media reach. Build an email list by offering useful material, a product sample, or access to an event in exchange for a voluntary subscription and appropriate consent to receive communications.

Attract subscribers from interested customers, workshops, and speaking events. Publish content that answers specific questions, demonstrates the results of your work, or teaches something practical. First, learn what genuinely attracts your audience. Only then invest in more elaborate marketing.

## Teach Through Writing and Public Speaking

---

If you want to write a book, decide who it is for, what you want to communicate, and why you have something valuable to say about the subject. Write for even 15 minutes a day. Worry about the title, cover, and publisher later. Once you prepare a book proposal, you can send it to publishers in your category and ask for feedback when they decline.

If you want to speak publicly, start with a Toastmasters club or a small workshop for interested people. Record your talks, review them, and improve one element at a time. Use your first workshops mainly to gain experience, collect feedback, and create material you can publish again later.

## Search for Work Proactively

---

Do not limit yourself to sending the same résumé everywhere. Choose companies that genuinely interest you, learn about their needs, and prepare a tailored application. If possible, include a work sample that shows what you can do for them.

Tell your network what kind of job you are looking for and what value you offer. Meet employers at industry events and initiate conversations rather than only waiting for job postings.

## Summary

---

First, decide how you want to live, then shape your work, goals, and daily responsibilities around that vision. Do not wait for perfect conditions: learn the basics, make a small attempt, and improve through experience.

Before investing in a business, verify that customers will actually pay. Build relationships, publish useful content, delegate gradually, and eliminate activities that do not move you toward your goals. The most important thing is not preparing to start, but actually getting started.
