# 6. Habits & 1%

---

Book: "Atomic Habits" By James Clear

## You Will Learn

---

- How to improve your life through small changes that compound over time.
- How to build and replace habits.
- How to use identity to make positive behaviors feel more natural.

## 1% better

---

Choose the areas of your life you want to work on at any given time. These could include your career, family, or home. Then aim to become 1% better every day.

Set aside some time each evening to reflect on what you could improve. Do not be afraid to experiment and test unconventional solutions. Remember that many changes take time to produce results, so do not give up.

## Identity

---

When looking for ways to improve, focus on who you want to become. Do not think of yourself as someone who is trying to change, but as someone who already is that person and simply acts accordingly.

This makes new habits feel more natural and increases the chance that they will stick. Instead of asking, “What should I do?”, ask, “What would a person like this do?”

Remember: you do what you are.

## What are habits?

---

Habits are actions that become automatic over time. They are like skills in a game: once unlocked, they support you throughout your entire life. Habits can shape what we do and how we do it, as well as how we think and behave.

A single habit may not change much on its own, but the effects of multiple habits compound. Step by step, you level up from Lv. 1 to Lv. 100, becoming capable of overcoming increasingly difficult challenges.

The key is not to unlock everything at once, but to consistently develop one branch at a time.

Each of us starts life with a different set of unlocked habits. We learn them by imitating our families and functioning within society. Some of these habits support us, while others hold us back.

Our goal is to build as many positive habits as possible and use them to overwrite the harmful ones, because once a skill has been learned, it cannot simply be deleted.

Missing a habit once does not matter much. The danger begins when one exception turns into a new pattern. If you miss once, make the next opportunity your priority. You do not need perfect consistency, but you should avoid failing twice in a row.

## How to Build a Habit

---

1. Choose the habit you want to build. Be specific.
2. Make it as simple as possible.
3. Reduce friction.
4. Repeat it as often as you can. The more often you repeat it, the faster it becomes automatic.

To make a new habit easier to build, do it immediately before or after a habit you already have. For example, take your supplements after brushing your teeth. Keep them right next to your toothbrush so they are visible and easy to remember.

Track your progress. Use a physical or digital habit tracker and check off the habit immediately after completing it. This gives you an immediate sense of progress and helps you see how consistent you actually are.

Remember, the more often you repeat a habit, the faster it becomes second nature. Missing an occasional day does not reset your progress. It only slows it down slightly.

## How to Replace a Bad Habit

---

1. Identify exactly what you want to change. Be specific.

2. Write down every situation that triggers the bad habit.

   For example, let's say you want to stop drinking sugary drinks. You may crave one when it is hot and you have not had enough water, after eating something heavy like pizza, or at a party where there is nothing better to drink.

3. Find a better alternative for each situation.

   Cold sparkling water can replace a sugary drink on a hot day. Tea can work better after a heavy meal. If you know there will be nothing good to drink at a party, bring your own alternative.

4. Make the alternatives extremely easy and the old habit difficult.

   Remove all sugary drinks from your home. Keep sparkling water cold in the fridge and put tea somewhere visible, preferably where you used to keep sugary drinks. Keep your water bottle clean and ready by the door.

The beginning will be the hardest, but the new behavior will gradually become easier. As you go, identify any additional situations that trigger the old habit and create better responses for them.

You can make bad habits artificially difficult and good habits more rewarding. For example, block access to social media and reward yourself for replacing scrolling with reading by adding money to a travel budget.

## Summary

---

Aim to become 1% better every day by looking for small improvements in the areas of your life that matter to you. Experiment, observe the results, and give changes enough time to work.

Act as the person you want to be and shape your habits around that identity. Build good habits by making them simple, easy, and repeatable. Replace bad habits by identifying their triggers, finding better alternatives, and making the old behavior harder to perform.

Small changes become habits, habits compound, and over time they can completely change the way you live.
