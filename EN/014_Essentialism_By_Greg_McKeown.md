# 14. Essentialism

---

Book: "Essentialism" by Greg McKeown

## Do Less, but Better

---

Do not try to do everything. The more projects and commitments you take on, the less time and energy you can devote to each one. Choose only what truly matters to you and commit to it fully.

To identify your priorities, answer three questions:

1. What am I passionate about?
2. Where can I use my talents?
3. How can I use them to help others?

Use your answers to define your main goal, and regularly check whether your actions are actually bringing you closer to it. Your goals may evolve, so adjust your priorities accordingly.

## Learn to Say No

---

Your time and energy are limited. If you do not decide how to use them, other people will decide for you.

Do not accept every opportunity just because it sounds interesting or because you do not want to disappoint someone. Say yes primarily to opportunities that align with your priorities. Consciously turn down the rest.

Set clear boundaries and do not let other people's expectations continually pull you away from your own goals. Every unnecessary commitment leaves less time for what truly matters.

## Regularly Eliminate What Is Unnecessary

---

Do not hold on to possessions, projects, or relationships simply because you have already invested time or money in them. Regularly ask yourself three questions:

1. If I did not own this item, would I buy it today?
2. If I had not started this project, would I still want to pursue it?
3. If I did not know this person, would I want to build a relationship with them today?

If the answer is no, consider whether it is still worth dedicating your resources to it.

Do not let past decisions automatically determine your future. Regularly remove what no longer adds enough value to your life.

## Create the Conditions for Effective Work

---

Find an environment where you can focus and work best. It might be a quiet room or a café. Use it regularly when working on your most important projects.

Build routines that make it easier to carry out your priorities without constantly making new decisions. Identify obstacles, such as a lack of time, distractions, or insufficient sleep, and gradually eliminate them.

Prioritize recovery. Getting enough sleep supports concentration, decision-making, and effective work.

## Summary

---

Do less, but better. Identify what truly matters to you and focus most of your energy on it. Do not accept commitments that distract you from your priorities.

Regularly reassess your projects, possessions, and relationships. Eliminate what no longer adds enough value, even if you have already invested time or money in it.

Build routines and an environment that support concentration, and make time for recovery. **Success is not about doing more and more, but about consistently doing the right things.**
