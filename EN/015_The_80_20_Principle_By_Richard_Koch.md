# 15. The 80/20 Principle

---

Book: "The 80/20 Principle" by Richard Koch

## The 80/20 Principle

---

Most results often come from a small portion of our actions. For example, 20% of your work may produce 80% of your results, while a small share of customers, investments, or decisions may generate most of the benefits. The 80/20 ratio is not a rigid rule, but a way to identify what creates the greatest value.

Do not measure your productivity by the number of hours you work. Working hard on unimportant tasks is no substitute for choosing your priorities wisely. **The goal is not to do more, but to do more of what actually works.**

## Focus on High-Value Activities

---

Regularly analyze which activities bring you the best results. Then spend more time on them and reduce the rest.

Prioritize tasks that move you toward your most important goals, use your strengths, require creativity, or can significantly improve how things are currently done.

Cut back on activities you perform only out of habit, unnecessary meetings, constant interruptions, and tasks that consume a lot of time while producing little value.

Look for ways to simplify, automate, and delegate work. You do not need to do everything yourself, especially when someone else can do it better.

## Surround Yourself with the Right People

---

Do not try to be the best at everything. Develop your strongest skills, and collaborate with people who have expertise in other areas.

In business, results often depend on a small group of exceptionally effective employees, partners, and customers. Identify these people, appreciate their contributions, and invest in building valuable relationships.

The same applies to your personal life. Not every relationship brings the same value. Give the most attention to relationships that matter to you, are mutual, and support your growth.

## Make Conscious Decisions

---

A small share of your decisions may account for most of your life's results. That is why you should devote the most attention to choices with long-term consequences.

Do not take action just because other people do it or recommend a particular path. Find out what actually works in your circumstances and look for your own solutions.

Apply this principle to investing, business, and everyday life as well. Identify the small set of activities that produce the greatest benefits and deliberately direct your resources toward them.

Remember, however, that you cannot save happiness for later the way you can save money. Regularly make time for experiences and relationships that bring you satisfaction instead of putting your whole life on hold until you achieve success.

## Summary

---

Most results often come from a small share of activities. Identify those that create the most value and focus your time, energy, and resources on them.

Eliminate unnecessary responsibilities, delegate tasks, and make use of your strongest skills. Surround yourself with capable people and invest in your most important relationships.

Do not confuse hard work with effectiveness. **Your goal is to achieve better results through fewer activities, not to keep increasing your effort.**
