# 9. Rejection

---

Book: "Rejection Proof" By Jia Jiang & his other content

## You Will Learn

---

- How to reduce your fear of rejection.
- Why rejection should not be taken personally.
- How to respond to rejection and learn from it.

## Do Not Reject Yourself

---

Do not assume that someone will say no before asking. If you avoid asking because you expect rejection, you reject yourself before the other person even gets the opportunity to decide. Ask for what you want clearly, politely, and respectfully, without demanding or acting entitled to a yes. Your responsibility is to ask. The other person's responsibility is to decide.

## Practice Rejection

---

Avoiding rejection makes the fear stronger, while facing it repeatedly makes it more familiar and less intimidating. Put yourself in situations where rejection is possible, starting with small requests and gradually increasing the difficulty. The goal is not to force yourself to enjoy rejection or eliminate every uncomfortable feeling, but to become comfortable enough with hearing no that fear no longer prevents you from asking.

## Rejection Is Not Personal

---

Rejection is never a personal attack. It reflects the other person's preferences, circumstances, needs, experiences, and perspective at that particular moment. The same request can receive a no from one person and a yes from another, so do not turn someone else's decision into a judgment about your value. Accept their decision respectfully and move forward without resentment.

## Stay After "No"

---

Do not automatically leave or become disappointed after hearing no. Stay friendly, relaxed, and curious. Smile, remain polite, and ask why without sounding hurt, defensive, or resentful. The purpose is not to challenge the person's decision or make them feel guilty, but to understand their perspective. A simple and friendly question can reveal useful information about why the request was rejected while keeping the interaction positive.

## Rejection Is an Opinion

---

Rejection is not an objective measure of whether your idea, work, request, or ability is good enough. People evaluate things through their own experiences, preferences, needs, expectations, and circumstances, which means different people can respond completely differently to the same request. One person's rejection is their response to a particular situation, not a universal truth about you or what you are offering.

## Make More Attempts

---

You control whether you ask, but you do not control the answer. Make requests politely and give people complete freedom to say either yes or no. The more meaningful attempts you make, the more rejection you will experience, but also the more opportunities you create. Do not measure your progress only by how often people say yes; pay attention to whether fear is preventing you from making requests you genuinely want to make.

## Summary

---

Do not reject yourself before giving other people the opportunity to decide. Rejection is not personal and does not determine your value. It reflects another person's perspective, preferences, needs, and circumstances. Instead of avoiding rejection, deliberately become more comfortable experiencing it. When you hear no, stay in the conversation, ask why, and learn from the answer. Focus on making meaningful attempts rather than controlling the outcome. The more willing you are to ask and hear no, the less fear determines your actions.
