# 8. Tidying Up

---

Book "The Life-Changing Magic of Tidying Up: The Japanese Art of Decluttering and Organizing" By Marie Kondo

## You Will Learn

---

- How to tidy up your home once and for all.
- How to decide what to throw away and what to keep.

## Tidying Up Once and for All

---

Your tidying session should start with throwing things away. The fewer things you have, the easier it is to keep everything organized. Set aside an entire day for tidying. Do it once and do it properly so that the chaos never comes back.

Get rid of as much as possible. Keep what brings you joy. Take an item in your hands and ask yourself: Does this bring me joy? Do not focus on its price or on the guilt you might feel about throwing it away.

Start with clothes, then books, papers, miscellaneous and finally sentimental items. By following this order, you will warm up with the easier things first. Gather all items of the same type in one place and only then start deciding what to keep.

Only after you have gotten rid of a large number of things and gone through everything you own should you start organizing. Every item must have its own place. Organize things by category. Keep all your clothes in one place, all your books in another, and so on. Do not leave empty spaces because you will start filling them with random things and the clutter will return.

When organizing your possessions, focus on practicality. Do not stack clothes on top of each other because they will be difficult to take out. Do not put everything on hangers either, because this takes up too much space. Store clothes and other items vertically so that they take up as little space as possible and are easy to take.

Do not keep large reserves of things for the future. Similarly, do not keep things simply because they might be useful someday. Nowadays, almost everything can be easily bought again, often for relatively little money.

## Summary

---

Declutter first and organize only after you have gone through everything you own. Keep what brings you joy and give every remaining item its own place.

Store things practically and avoid keeping unnecessary reserves or possessions that you might need someday. The fewer things you own, the easier it is to maintain order.
