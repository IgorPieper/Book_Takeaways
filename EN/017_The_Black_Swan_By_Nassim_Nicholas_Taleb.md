# 17. The Black Swan

---

Book: "The Black Swan" by Nassim Nicholas Taleb

## The Black Swan

---

People naturally look for simple explanations and predictable patterns. We assume that the future will resemble the past and that the world will change in ways we can foresee. Yet the biggest changes often come from events that nobody expected.

A black swan is an event with three defining characteristics:

1. **Unpredictability:** It is so unusual that our past experience does not allow us to predict it reliably.
2. **Enormous impact:** It has consequences that can transform entire economies, industries, or individual lives.
3. **The illusion of predictability:** After it happens, people create explanations that make the event seem obvious and predictable in hindsight.

Examples of events illustrating the black swan concept:

- The outbreak of World War I.
- Hitler's rise to power.
- The collapse of the Soviet Union.
- The emergence of the Internet.
- Google's dominance.
- The introduction of the iPhone and the rise of smartphones.
- The COVID-19 pandemic.
- The emergence of ChatGPT and the rapid development of AI.

Black swans can be either negative or positive. Do not assume something cannot happen simply because you have never experienced it before.

## Two Worlds: The Mediocre and the Extremists

---

**The mediocre** are people who follow the majority. They earn average incomes, lead average lives, and choose conventional career paths. Their days look alike, and what matters most to them is stability, peace, and immediate pleasure. They prepare for events they can predict, without expecting radical changes.

**Extremists** are people who choose their own path. They challenge established patterns, take unconventional actions, and create opportunities that can lead to unpredictable, extraordinary results.

The growth of the Internet and new technologies makes it easier than ever to move from an average life to achieving extreme results. Today, a single person can create a product, company, or piece of content that reaches millions of people without having enormous resources.

**The number of black swans is increasing year after year, and the pace at which they appear will only accelerate.** Technological development means that radical changes can occur faster and faster, so you need to be prepared for a world in which the rules you rely on today may stop applying at any moment.

## Do Not Overestimate Your Knowledge

---

People naturally look for patterns and explanations, even where chance plays a major role. This leads to several cognitive biases:

- **Confirmation bias:** We seek information that supports our beliefs while ignoring evidence that contradicts them.
- **Narrative fallacy:** We create logical stories to explain the past, underestimating the role of chance and uncertainty.
- **Survivorship bias:** We examine the stories of successful people and companies while forgetting thousands of similar attempts that ended in failure.

Consider a turkey that receives food from a farmer every day. With each passing day, it becomes more convinced that the farmer keeps it safe. Then one day, just before Thanksgiving, everything changes.

Similarly, we mostly observe people who appear in the media, ignoring those working outside our field of view. We hear success stories that often leave out the daily struggles, failures, and chance circumstances that led to the final outcome.

**A long history of success does not guarantee safety in the future.** Look for evidence that could disprove your beliefs, and ask yourself what your analysis might be missing.

## Do Not Rely on Forecasts

---

The more complex a system and the further into the future we look, the harder it is to make reliable predictions. Experts often present highly precise forecasts even though they cannot account for every important variable.

Do not confuse confidence with forecasting accuracy. Even a complicated mathematical model can overlook the greatest risks.

Instead of trying to predict a specific event, think about the consequences if it happened. You do not need to know when a crisis will occur to prepare for the possibility of losing your income.

**Focus on preparing for different scenarios rather than making your future depend on a single forecast.**

## Build Resilience to the Unpredictable

---

You cannot eliminate uncertainty, but you can limit its negative consequences.

Avoid situations in which a single mistake could lead to disaster. Limit excessive debt, maintain financial reserves, and do not make your entire life or business dependent on one client, product, or source of income.

Taleb also proposes the **barbell strategy**. It combines a very safe foundation with a small number of risky experiments that offer large potential gains while keeping possible losses limited.

Instead of risking everything for one big opportunity, protect your basic position and devote a small portion of your resources to experimentation.

The most important thing is to survive unfavorable events without losing the ability to keep going.

## Increase Your Chances of Positive Black Swans

---

Not every surprise is a threat. An unexpected connection, idea, or business opportunity can produce results far beyond your initial expectations.

Look for situations in which the potential reward is enormous and the cost of failure is small. Meet new people, experiment with ideas, publish your work, and explore different opportunities.

You do not have to predict which attempt will become a major success. You only need to keep creating opportunities for one to happen.

Stay flexible. Do not become so attached to one plan that you cannot take advantage of a better opportunity when it appears unexpectedly.

## Summary

---

The biggest changes often result from events we could not predict. **The number of black swans is increasing year after year, and the pace at which they appear will only accelerate.** Do not assume the future will resemble the past, and do not overestimate your knowledge or that of experts.

Do not blindly follow the majority. Challenge established patterns, choose your own path, and look for opportunities to achieve extraordinary results.

Build resilience to unexpected events. Avoid risks that could ruin you, maintain reserves, and protect your basic needs.

At the same time, increase your exposure to positive surprises. Run many small experiments, meet people, and look for opportunities with high potential. **You do not need to predict the future if you can prepare for its uncertainty and take advantage of opportunities as they arise.**
