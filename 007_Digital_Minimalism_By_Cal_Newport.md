# 7. Digital Minimalism

---

Book: "Digital Minimalism" By Cal Newport

## You Will Learn

---

- How to reduce the hidden cost of technology without abandoning it completely.
- How to identify which digital tools genuinely improve your life.
- How to reclaim your attention, relationships, and free time.

## The Hidden Cost of Technology

---

Technology makes our lives easier, but it also carries a significant hidden cost. It consumes enormous amounts of time, fragments our attention, and changes the way we experience relationships.

The goal is not to eliminate technology completely. That would be unrealistic for most people. Instead, consciously choose which technologies you use, why you use them, and when they are allowed to enter your life.

Technology should serve your values rather than determine how you spend your time.

## The Digital Declutter

---

If you want to see how much your life can change without unnecessary technology, try a 30-day digital declutter. For one month, remove all optional technologies from your life. Keep only what is essential, such as phone calls, navigation, banking, and tools required for work. Even then, look for ways to minimize their use.

Focus on elimination first. Keep a technology only when you can clearly explain the value it provides. If two tools serve the same purpose, remove the weaker one. At the end of the month, reintroduce technologies one at a time. Before bringing something back, answer three questions:

1. What specific value does this technology provide?
2. Is it the best way to receive that value?
3. What rules will prevent it from taking more time than it deserves?

Do not allow a technology back into your life simply because it is useful. Almost every tool is useful in some way. It should offer enough value to justify its cost.

## Small Habits

---

1. Allow yourself to experience boredom and solitude. Create moments when your mind can wander without receiving new information. These moments help you process emotions, develop original ideas, strengthen concentration, and solve problems. When every pause is filled with a screen, your brain loses the space required for deeper thinking.

2. Go for walks and leave your phone at home. Pay attention to your surroundings, your thoughts, and the physical experience of walking. Without notifications competing for your attention, your mind can slow down, process unresolved thoughts, and generate new ideas. You return with less mental noise, better concentration, and a clearer perspective on problems that may have felt overwhelming before the walk.

3. Spend intentional time with other people. Minimize social media contact and maximize face-to-face conversations. When you have fewer opportunities to meet others, write in a physical journal instead of automatically reaching for your phone.

4. Silence notifications and choose specific times to respond to messages. Do not reply during every free moment. This protects your attention and teaches other people that you are not constantly available.

5. Avoid endless social media scrolling. In particular, do not leave likes, hearts, or other reactions under posts, and avoid commenting. These actions trick your brain into treating shallow interactions as genuine social communication, even though they lack the depth of a real conversation. They create a false sense of connection and can reduce your motivation to call someone, have a meaningful conversation, or meet in person.

## Free Time

---

Prioritize physical activities over passive time online. Create something, exercise, learn a practical skill, or meet other people to do something together. Plan your free time in advance. Decide what you want to do before entertainment platforms make that decision for you. Set clear limits for social media and choose activities that challenge you or help you grow. Spend this time consciously. Give your full attention to what you are doing instead of constantly switching between the activity and your phone.

At least once a week, build, repair, or improve something with your hands. Cook a meal, fix an object, work in a garden, or create something useful. Do not spend all your free time consuming what other people have created. Develop the ability to influence the physical world around you.

## Summary

---

Technology should be a tool that supports your values, not a force that controls your attention.

Remove optional technologies for 30 days and observe how your life changes. Reintroduce only the tools whose value you can clearly identify, and create strict rules for how you use them.

Protect moments of boredom and solitude. Walk without your phone, meet people in person, limit notifications, and stop filling every empty moment with scrolling.

Use your free time to create, move, learn, repair, and connect with others. The less time you spend consuming digital distractions, the more time you gain for building a meaningful life.
